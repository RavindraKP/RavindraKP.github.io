Digital Current Affairs platform: relaunch, growth and premium

A bilingual current-affairs product for UPSC aspirants, from daily news to yearly compilations, with an editorial back office where AI drafts and editors publish.

730K → 5Musers in about a year after the relaunch
~₹1 crorepremium revenue a year
4%free-to-paid conversion
1m 12s → 5m 35stime per user

The problem

Current affairs is the most frequently refreshed part of UPSC preparation: daily news, weekly and monthly digests, yearly compilations, the Budget and the Economic Survey.

The content existed in many places and formats. Students needed one place to read, revise and practise it, and the business needed a digital product people would pay for.

Who it serves

  • UPSC aspirants reading in English or Hindi.
  • The editorial team: 67 users across 32 permission roles and 20+ content lines.

My role

I led the relaunch: product scope, content workflows, positioning and the user experience. I designed the free-trial-to-paid model, set pricing and plans, and worked on the payment gateway integration. I tracked adoption and conversion through GA4 and Rybbit.

How it works

  1. Newspapers scanned through the day
  2. Articles scored for UPSC relevance
  3. AI drafts summaries and practice questions
  4. Editor reviews and publishesHuman review
  5. Students read, take notes, listen and practise
  6. Each published article indexed for the AI assistant

Step 4 is the human review point.

Key decisions and trade-offs

AI drafts, editors publish

Generation runs in the background and notifies the editor. Nothing reaches students without editorial approval, which protected trust in a product students rely on for facts.

Free trial before paywall

Students try the full product before paying. The model converts about 4% of users to paid and brings in about ₹1 crore a year, without aggressive paid marketing.

One back office for every format

One admin panel handles daily, weekly, monthly and yearly content, quizzes, plans and permissions, so a new content line does not need a new tool.

Meet students where they study

Hindi and English versions, audio versions of articles, notes and bookmarks were part of the relaunch, over a period in which time per user rose from 1 minute 12 seconds to 5 minutes 35 seconds.

How quality is measured

  • Editorial approval gates on summaries, published news and practice quizzes.
  • Question acceptance rate as the quality measure for generated questions, raised from about 50% to 80%.
  • Engagement measured as time per user, views and repeat visits; conversion tracked from trial to paid.

Outcome

  • Users grew from 730K to 5M, and views from 3.6M to 29.4M, in about a year after the relaunch.
  • About ₹1 crore a year in premium revenue from 4,833 subscriptions, at about 4% conversion, without aggressive paid marketing.
  • Time per user rose from 1 minute 12 seconds to 5 minutes 35 seconds.

Beyond education

Trial-led subscription design and AI-assisted publishing apply to any content or media business, and to B2B SaaS with a free-trial funnel.
