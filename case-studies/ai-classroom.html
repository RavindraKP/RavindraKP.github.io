AI in Classroom: lecture-to-learning-content pipeline

Turns every recorded lecture into a detailed summary, a short revision note, an MCQ quiz and a subjective assignment, reviewed by a small team before students see it.

Role: Product leadPeriod: 2022 to 2024Areas: Transcription, LLM pipelines, Bilingual content, Reviewer workflow, GCP
10,000+students served daily
24–48h → 4–6hcontent turnaround per lecture
~60 → 5–6people needed for review
200+NCERT lectures with AI content

The problem

Each classroom lecture needed a summary, revision notes, a quiz and an assignment. Writing these by hand took one to two days per lecture and a team of about 60 people.

Students wanted the material while the lecture was still fresh.

Who it serves

  • Classroom programme students on the learning platform.
  • Content reviewers and batch coordinators.

My role

I led the product: defined the output set for each lecture, the review workflow and the controls coordinators needed, and worked with engineering on the pipeline and its monitoring.

How it works

  1. Lecture video recorded
  2. Transcribed
  3. Detailed class summary
  4. Short revision summary
  5. MCQ quiz of 10 to 16 questions
  6. One subjective assignment
  7. Reviewer approves
  8. Published to the platform

Step 7 is the human review point. Failures at any stage raise an alert to the team.

Key decisions and trade-offs

Keep a reviewer, shrink the team

AI drafts every output, and a small review team approves it. Quality stayed under human control while the work changed from writing to checking.

Bilingual from the start

English and Hindi outputs are generated in the same run, so Hindi-medium batches are not served late.

Give coordinators batch-level controls

Coordinators can switch outputs on or off per batch and watch transcription status, so one failed lecture does not hold up a batch.

Extend, don't rebuild

NCERT in Classroom reused the same pipeline for 200+ lectures and added a chatbot over lectures, NCERT books, current affairs and past papers.

How quality is measured

  • Every output passes reviewer approval before publication.
  • Transcription monitoring and failure alerts catch broken runs before students notice.
  • The shared question-generation engine raised question acceptance from about 50% to 80% through prompt, model and evaluation changes.

Outcome

  • Turnaround per lecture down from 24–48 hours to 4–6 hours.
  • Review team down from about 60 people to 5–6.
  • Serves 10,000+ students every day.

What I would do differently

  • Log reviewer edits per output type, so the weakest output gets prompt work first.
  • Ask students to rate each summary and quiz, to measure usefulness, not only correctness.
